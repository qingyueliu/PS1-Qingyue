Strategic Reports and Cooperation

How do uncertain third-party reports affect cooperation? Compare honesty incentives with a reward for A's later cooperation.

Shared assumptions

Except in round 1, C observes B in a preceding C–B interaction. C reports that past action before A and B play. Pairing stays fixed. An honest report about the past is not a guarantee of B's next action.

Payoffs: mutual cooperation = 3/3; unilateral defection = 4/0; mutual defection = 1/1.

C's reporting reward = honesty reward or minus lying cost, plus the bonus if A then cooperates in the market-linked condition. This external bonus does not reduce A's payoff. Social welfare below measures A+B only. A's cooperation can help or expose A to exploitation; rewarding it does not guarantee mutual benefit.

Part 1 · Agent-only experiment

A, B, and C make decisions with Q-learning. You set the number of rounds and see only the aggregate results.

The five levels mean 2%, 5%, 12%, 25%, or 40% probability of exploring a random action at each decision; otherwise an agent chooses its current best learned action. They measure exploration, not intelligence or training progress. Browser runs are unseeded educational demonstrations; the repository notebook provides the 30-seed research comparison.

Part 2 · Play as Agent A

Before each decision after round 1, C reports B's actual choice from their immediately preceding interaction. C can report truthfully or lie.

Learning randomness is the probability that an agent explores a random action instead of choosing its current best learned action: Very low = 2%, Low = 5%, Medium = 12%, High = 25%, and Very high = 40%. A higher level means more exploration and less predictable play.
