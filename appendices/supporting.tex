\section{Technical details and reproducibility}
\subsection*{Related-work positioning}
Leung et al.~\cite{leung2026} study independent RL agents that use minimal direct and third-party observations while selecting interaction partners. Their model establishes the baseline role of limited observation and partner selection. This proposal keeps A--B matching, Prisoner's Dilemma payoffs, and the original learning settings fixed; it defers partner selection and changes only C's reporting incentive by adding a bonus after A cooperates. It is a controlled incentive extension that asks whether C's private gain improves the report's usefulness and A+B welfare. The present code reconstructs this educational A--B--C model and does not reproduce Leung et al.'s population or interaction-network model.

\subsection*{Model and evaluation details}
The model retains A/B payoffs $(3,3)$, $(0,4)$, $(4,0)$, and $(1,1)$. After round 1, C and B first play an exposure interaction; C observes B's exposure action and reports before the focal A--B interaction. C receives $+0.4$ for truth and $-0.2$ for lying, plus $\beta=1$ if A later cooperates in the new \texttt{market\_linked} condition. The matched strategic baseline has $\beta=0$. Reporting rewards are credited after A acts in both conditions. C's exposure payoff trains its separate action table; it is not counted in its reported mean reporting reward.

Action learning uses $\alpha=.12$, $\gamma=.85$; report learning uses $\alpha_C=.15$, $\gamma_C=.80$. The update remains the v1 rule $Q(a)\leftarrow Q(a)+\alpha[r+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(a')-Q(a)]$, bootstrapping within the selected table rather than a separately modeled next state. B and the truth/lie reporter have stateless tables; A has separate action tables for no signal, reported cooperation, and reported defection. Ties choose the first action. This simple learning heuristic cannot represent all history-dependent repeated-game strategies. The player demo retains its original $.15/.80$ B/C action-learning settings; it is educational, not a human experiment or a numerical replication of the agent-only panel.

Exploration levels are $.02,.05,.12,.25,.40$, with $.12$ the default. At each choice, probability $\epsilon$ selects a random action; otherwise the current maximizing action is selected. These are exploration rates, not intelligence or learning-progress levels. Mutual cooperation, exploitation (exactly one defector), and welfare refer to the focal A--B interaction; welfare excludes exposure payoffs and the external reporting bonus. A's own cooperation and C's reporting reward are reported separately. Honesty measures fidelity to the exposure action; predictive accuracy measures agreement with B's subsequent focal action. Reports have denominator $T-1$; focal outcomes have denominator $T$.

The v2 panel uses seeds 0--29 and 10,000 rounds for each of five conditions at five exploration rates: 750 runs. Conditions are no report, strategic report, verified truth with forced use, bonus-linked report, and the retained high-lying-cost diagnostic ($\ell=1$). The verified condition changes both truthfulness and report use, so its contrast is not a pure information-quality effect. The high-cost diagnostic is retained for continuity with v1, not introduced as a second new mechanism. The code is a reconstruction of the educational model, not a reproduction of Leung et al.'s population/partner-selection model~\cite{leung2026}.

Across-seed cooperation SDs are 3.74, 1.62, 1.83, 2.17, and 1.62 percentage points in table order. The bonus-linked minus strategic contrast is $-.082$ percentage points with approximate 95\% interval $[-.652,.489]$. Honesty changes by $-.525$ points $[-.755,-.296]$, and A+B welfare by $-.0040$ $[-.0296,.0215]$. C's reporting reward increases from $.364$ to $.458$. Intervals use the seed-paired differences: mean $\pm1.96\,s/\sqrt{30}$, with sample SD $s$; they are descriptive normal approximations without multiple-comparison correction. Equal seed labels do not imply identical random-number use across unlike treatment branches.

At $\epsilon=.02$, strategic and bonus-linked cooperation average 21.99\% and 22.19\% over all rounds, but 2.88\% and 2.95\% in the final 20\%. No-report cooperation has a large SD (37.89 points). These transients and seed variation prevent a universal convergence claim. At higher exploration rates, random actions also contribute to observed cooperation. Full per-seed data, final-20\% outcomes, and all five-level results are in \texttt{companion/outputs/ps1\_revision\_panel.json}; the executed notebook visualizes sensitivity.

For the stage game, expected payoffs differ by one for every belief about B. This does not rule out continuation incentives in richer repeated games. For C, holding continuation values fixed, lying rather than truth is attractive only if $\beta[\Pr(A=C\mid\mathrm{lie})-\Pr(A=C\mid\mathrm{truth})]>.6$. This one-period condition explains the possible incentive conflict; the simulation does not assume that lying actually achieves it. An A-cooperation bonus is not a welfare bonus, and its funding is outside the model.

My Week 1 claim treated reports mainly as true or false. Field observations and the CodeCarbon and OpenFisca examples led me to distinguish honesty from accuracy, completeness, and verifiability. Preparing Session G separated three mechanisms: Feiyu Li's economics contribution emphasized incentive credibility, Yitong Lin's computer-science contribution emphasized detecting planned deception, and my behavioral-science contribution emphasized strategic uncertainty. On September 9, the longer simulation runs challenged my expectation that high honesty would preserve cooperation: honesty remained near 94\%, while cooperation and welfare fell. I therefore changed the proposal from ``does lying destroy cooperation?'' to ``when does credible information become behaviorally useful?'' and added controlled information conditions and a social-planner welfare benchmark.

My earlier aspiration was to develop a game-theoretic allocation system that is fair, efficient, sustainable, and difficult to manipulate. The revised statement narrows the unresolved boundary to auditable allocation when decisions depend on strategic third-party information. \subsection*{V2 cumulative reflection: September 20, 2026}
Yichen Shen's question made me distinguish a message changing a belief from a message changing an incentive. I retained fixed matching so the project continues to study third-party reporting, and deferred his partner-selection comparison because it changes the action space. Zhengjun He's suggestion supplied a feasible revision within that boundary: C can now benefit from A's later cooperation. The resulting comparison shows why rewarding the observer for a seemingly desirable action need not improve market welfare. C's own reward rose, but the cooperation and welfare contrasts did not provide clear evidence of improvement.

Prof. Luyao Zhang's separate methodological feedback encouraged truthful-observer benchmarks, multiple seeds, and changing one incentive at a time. I adopted that discipline without presenting it as a third peer review or claiming to have reproduced the cited observer paper. The five exploration levels revealed substantial low-exploration transients. I therefore narrowed my claim to the specified learning rules, horizons, and settings, and separated honesty, predictive accuracy, report use, and mutual cooperation. This develops the same research question rather than replacing it with partner-choice design.
