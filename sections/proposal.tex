\section{Three connected research questions}
Leung et al.~\cite{leung2026} study RL agents using minimal third-party observation and partner selection to support cooperation. I use their work as the baseline without reproducing its population or interaction-network model. Holding A--B matching, payoffs, and learning settings fixed, I change only C's reporting incentive: a bonus after A cooperates. This controlled incentive extension asks whether C's private reward improves report usefulness, cooperation, and A+B welfare; partner selection remains future work. C observes B, reports to A, and A chooses whether to use the report before playing B. Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} links three perspectives:

\textbf{Q1---Economics:} Does rewarding C for A's subsequent cooperation align C's benefit with A+B welfare?\par
\textbf{Q2---Computation:} How do reporting incentives affect honesty, report use, and cooperation across learning settings?\par
\textbf{Q3---Behavior:} Does report use indicate useful information, or merely payoff learning?

The direction remains strategic third-party information in a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma; v2 isolates one observer incentive.

\section{Answer to Q1: incentives and intellectual lineage}
After round 1, C observes B's action $s$ in a C--B exposure stage, sends $m$, and A uses or ignores it before A--B play. A/B payoffs remain $(3,3),(0,4),(4,0),(1,1)$. Following \emph{Zhengjun He's} suggestion, C's reporting reward is
\[
r_C=0.4\mathbf{1}(m=s)-0.2\mathbf{1}(m\ne s)
     +\beta\mathbf{1}(a_A=C),
\]
with $\beta=0$ in the strategic baseline and $\beta=1$ in the added condition. The external bonus does not change A/B payoffs; A+B welfare excludes it.

Addressing \emph{Yichen Shen}, if $p$ is B's cooperation probability, A's expected stage payoffs are $3p$ for C and $1+3p$ for D. Better information changes $p$, not this one-point gap. This is a stage-game result, not an impossibility theorem for repeated cooperation. Harsanyi's incomplete-information framework~\cite{harsanyi1968} and strategic communication~\cite{crawford1982} motivate separating reporting incentives from receiver benefits. Pairing stays fixed; partner selection is deferred.

\section{Answer to Q2: method and evaluation}
Tabular Q-learning~\cite{watkins1992} retains v1 parameters. The new comparison changes only C's bonus, credited after A acts. Thirty seeds, 10,000 rounds, and five exploration rates test sensitivity (Appendix A).

At $\epsilon=.12$, strategic versus bonus-linked reporting gives 93.98\% versus 93.46\% honesty and 2.75\% versus 2.66\% mutual cooperation. C's reporting reward rises from .364 to .458, while the cooperation difference is $-0.082$ percentage points (approximate 95\% interval $[-.652,.489]$). There is no clear cooperation improvement. At $\epsilon=.02$, strategic cooperation averages 21.99\%; conclusions depend on exploration and horizon. All findings are synthetic.

\section{Answer to Q3: behavior and competing explanations}
Gossip can affect cooperation~\cite{sommerfeld2007}, but using a report need not mean cooperating or feeling trust. C reports a past action: honesty differs from predicting B's later action. Under the default strategic condition these rates are 93.98\% and 83.73\%. The verified benchmark forces both truthful reporting and report use, so it does not isolate credibility alone.

Zhengjun's proposed payoff link tests whether C benefits when A cooperates; Yichen's question tests why A should respond. The observed rise in C's reward without a clear A+B welfare gain separates private benefit from social usefulness. Q-values measure learned action values, not human beliefs. No human behavioral effect is established.

\section{Advanced development and future directions}
The 1994 economics prize recognized Nash, Harsanyi, and Selten's equilibrium analysis~\cite{nobel1994}; Sutton and Barto received the 2024 Turing Award for reinforcement learning~\cite{turing2024}. Today's distinctive problem is agents learning from other strategic agents' reports. The next test varies bonus strength and run length under fixed matching; partner selection remains a separate future extension.
